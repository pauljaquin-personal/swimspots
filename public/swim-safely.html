Swim safely

Open water changes quickly. A good swim starts with checking the place, the conditions and your own limits.

Before you get in

Look at the water, check the weather and read local signs. Know where you will enter and get out, and have another exit in mind if conditions change.

Cold water

New Zealand lakes, rivers and the sea can be cold even on warm days. Enter progressively, allow time to settle your breathing, and shorten the swim if you are becoming cold, clumsy or confused.

Wind and weather

Wind can make a return leg much harder than the outward swim and can build chop quickly. Check the forecast, but also pay attention to what is happening at the water when you arrive.

Rivers and currents

River flow, depth and underwater hazards can change after rain. In moving water, understand where the current goes before entering and avoid relying on a familiar spot behaving the same way every day.

Sea, tides and swell

Tides, swell, surf and currents can change access and swimming conditions. Check local coastal information and be particularly cautious around rocks, channels, river mouths and exposed beaches.

Water quality

Check the latest official advice where monitoring is available. Water quality can deteriorate after rainfall, and a recent sample does not guarantee conditions at every point along a beach, lake or river.

Visibility and company

Make yourself easy to see where boats or other water users may be present. Swimming with other people, or having someone know your plan, adds an important layer of safety.

Local knowledge matters

Follow signs, closures and advice at the location. If what you see does not match what you expected from a forecast or this website, treat the conditions in front of you as the more important information.

Swimspots helps you find and understand places to swim; it cannot tell you that a particular swim is safe. Conditions, access and individual capability vary. In an emergency in New Zealand, call 111.
